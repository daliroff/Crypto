# Lesson 03: Support and Resistance

## Module 3 — Technical Analysis | Crypto Trading: Zero to Hero

---

## Introduction

Support and resistance are the most fundamental concepts in all of technical analysis. Every chart pattern, every indicator, every trading strategy ultimately comes back to understanding where price is likely to find buyers (support) and where it's likely to find sellers (resistance). Master this concept and you'll have a framework that works across every crypto asset, every timeframe, and every market condition.

---

## What Is Support?

**Support** is a price level — or zone — at which buying interest is strong enough to halt a declining price and potentially reverse it upward. Think of support as a floor beneath the market.

When price approaches support:
- Buyers who missed a previous low see an opportunity to enter.
- Traders who are short begin taking profits (which means buying back their short position).
- Long-term holders add to their positions at perceived value.
- This collective buying pressure exceeds selling pressure, causing price to stabilize or bounce.

The more times a support level has held, the more significant it becomes. A level that has bounced price three times is far more important than one that has only held once.

---

## What Is Resistance?

**Resistance** is a price level at which selling pressure exceeds buying pressure, causing upward momentum to stall or reverse. Think of resistance as a ceiling above the market.

When price approaches resistance:
- Traders who bought at lower levels sell to lock in profits.
- Traders who bought near the top at a previous high and are "trapped" (underwater) sell as soon as they break even.
- Short sellers initiate new positions anticipating a reversal.
- This selling pressure overwhelms buyers and causes price to stall or fall.

---

## How Support and Resistance Levels Form

S/R levels don't appear randomly — they form in predictable ways:

### Previous Highs and Lows
The most reliable S/R levels are formed by previous significant price extremes:
- A price level that previously acted as a major high (resistance) will often attract selling again when price returns to it.
- A price level that previously acted as a significant low (support) will often attract buying again.
- **Example:** BTC's previous all-time high of roughly $20,000 (December 2017) became a critical resistance level years later during the 2020-2021 bull run — and then became a major support test during the 2022 bear market.

### Round Numbers and Psychological Levels
Human psychology is drawn to round numbers. In crypto:
- $10,000 / $20,000 / $30,000 / $50,000 / $100,000 for BTC
- $1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 / $4,000 for ETH
- $100 / $200 / $50 for SOL

These levels attract massive attention from retail and institutional traders alike, making them self-fulfilling support/resistance zones. BTC spent weeks consolidating around $30,000 multiple times — pure psychology at work.

### Consolidation Zones
When price trades sideways for an extended period, a large number of positions are opened at that price range. This creates a **zone** (not just a line) of support or resistance, because all those traders have a reference price in that area and will react when price returns to it.

### High-Volume Nodes
Levels where enormous volume has traded historically act as magnets. Price tends to return to these areas because so many people have positions anchored there.

---

## Role Reversal: When Support Becomes Resistance (and Vice Versa)

One of the most powerful concepts in technical analysis is **role reversal** — a broken support level becomes future resistance, and a broken resistance level becomes future support.

**The logic:** Imagine BTC consolidating at $40,000 for weeks (support). When price breaks below $40,000, all the traders who bought at $40,000 are now at a loss. When price eventually rallies back to $40,000, those trapped buyers will sell to break even — turning the old support into new resistance.

Conversely, if BTC breaks above $50,000 resistance, the traders who were selling there missed the move up. When price pulls back to $50,000, those same traders (and new ones who recognize the level) will buy — turning old resistance into new support.

**Example:** ETH broke above $2,000 resistance in early 2021. In subsequent pullbacks, $2,000 repeatedly acted as support. When ETH eventually crashed below $2,000 in 2022, that level flipped back to resistance.

---

## How to Draw S/R Lines Correctly

There is ongoing debate about whether to use candle **wicks** or candle **bodies** when drawing S/R:

- **Using bodies:** The body (Open-to-Close range) represents where price spent the most time and where most trading occurred. Drawing S/R through bodies shows where the real conviction was.
- **Using wicks:** Wicks show the extreme price tested. Drawing S/R at wicks shows the absolute price extreme that was rejected.

**Best practice:** Use **zones**, not precise lines. A support zone from $29,800 to $30,200 is more realistic than pretending there's an exact line at $30,000. Price rarely respects a single price to the penny.

For drawing: zoom out to a weekly or daily chart, identify the obvious previous highs/lows, and draw horizontal lines (or rectangles for zones) at those levels.

---

## Confluence Zones: Multiple Timeframe Alignment

A support or resistance level becomes far more powerful when it aligns across **multiple timeframes**. This is called **confluence**.

For example, if $1,800 on ETH is:
- A daily chart support level (previous low)
- A 4-hour chart support level (consolidation zone)
- A weekly chart trendline support
- AND a round psychological number

...then you have four confluent reasons why that level should hold. Confluence-based trades have significantly higher win rates than isolated signals.

## Dynamic Support and Resistance: Moving Averages

Not all S/R is horizontal. **Moving averages** act as dynamic S/R levels that move with price:
- The **200-day moving average** is the most widely watched dynamic support in all of crypto. BTC trading above its 200 DMA is generally considered a bull market; below is a bear market.
- The **50-day moving average** frequently acts as support in bull markets — BTC and ETH will often dip to the 50 DMA and bounce.
- **Example:** During the 2021 bull run, BTC repeatedly bounced off the 21-week exponential moving average, which acted as dynamic support throughout the entire trend.

---

## Two Trading Approaches: Bounce vs. Breakout

Once you identify a key S/R level, you have two strategies:

### Trading the Bounce
- **Entry:** Buy near support (or sell near resistance) anticipating a reversal.
- **Stop-loss:** Placed just below support (or above resistance). If the level breaks, you're wrong and exit quickly.
- **Target:** The opposite S/R level or a predetermined risk/reward ratio.
- **Best for:** Ranging markets, well-tested levels, confluence zones.

### Trading the Breakout
- **Entry:** Buy as price closes above resistance (or sell as price closes below support), confirming the break.
- **Stop-loss:** Placed just below the broken resistance level (now support via role reversal).
- **Target:** The next significant S/R level above.
- **Risk:** False breakouts are common, especially on low volume.
- **Best for:** Trending markets, breakouts with high volume confirmation.

---

## Stop Placement Relative to S/R

Where you place your stop-loss relative to S/R levels is critical to professional trading:

- **Never place stops exactly at the S/R line.** Price often temporarily wicks through a level before reversing. A stop right at $30,000 will get hit by a brief dip to $29,900 even if the level ultimately holds.
- **Place stops a meaningful distance beyond the level** — enough to avoid being stopped out by normal market noise. The typical guideline is 1-3% beyond the level, depending on volatility.
- **Example:** If you're buying BTC at the $42,000 support zone, place your stop at $40,500 — below the zone, not at its exact bottom edge.

---

## Real Example: BTC and the $20,000 Psychological Level

One of the most dramatic S/R examples in crypto history centers on BTC's $20,000 level:

- **December 2017:** BTC reached an all-time high of roughly $20,000 before crashing. This became the defining resistance level.
- **December 2020:** After 3 years of acting as resistance, BTC finally broke above $20,000 on massive volume. Role reversal: it immediately became support.
- **2021:** Multiple pullbacks to $20,000 found strong buying.
- **June 2022:** The catastrophic bear market broke $20,000 to the downside. The old support became fierce resistance.
- **January 2023 – 2024:** $20,000 acted as resistance during the early recovery before BTC broke above it again.

Three years of trading around a single price level, driven entirely by the power of support, resistance, and role reversal.

## Key Takeaways

> **Lesson 03 — Support and Resistance**
>
> - **Support** is a price floor where buying pressure prevents further decline. **Resistance** is a price ceiling where selling pressure prevents further advance.
> - S/R levels form at **previous highs/lows**, **round numbers** ($30K, $50K), and **consolidation zones** where a large volume of positions were opened.
> - **Role reversal** is one of the most reliable concepts in TA: broken support becomes resistance, broken resistance becomes support.
> - Draw S/R as **zones**, not precise lines. Price rarely respects a single price to the penny.
> - **Confluence** — when multiple S/R factors align at the same level (different timeframes, round numbers, MAs) — creates the highest-probability trade setups.
> - **Moving averages** (especially the 200 DMA) act as dynamic support/resistance that moves with price.
> - Two core strategies: **trade the bounce** (reversal at S/R) or **trade the breakout** (continuation through S/R with volume confirmation).
> - Always place stops **beyond** S/R levels, not at them, to avoid getting stopped out by normal wicks.
