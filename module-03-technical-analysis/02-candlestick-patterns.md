## Introduction

Candlestick charts were invented by Japanese rice traders in the 18th century. Today they are the universal language of technical traders across every market — including crypto. Understanding candlestick patterns gives you insight into the psychological battle between buyers and sellers playing out in real time. This lesson covers the most reliable single-candle, two-candle, and three-candle patterns used by professional crypto traders.

---

## Candlestick Anatomy

Before studying patterns, you need to understand the structure of a single candlestick:

- **Body:** The wide rectangular section of the candle, representing the range between the Open and Close prices.
  - **Green (or white) body:** The Close is higher than the Open. Buyers were in control during this period. Called a **bullish candle**.
  - **Red (or black) body:** The Close is lower than the Open. Sellers were in control. Called a **bearish candle**.
- **Upper Wick (Upper Shadow):** The thin line extending above the body. It shows the highest price reached during the period. A long upper wick means buyers pushed price up, but sellers rejected it — pushing price back down before the candle closed.
- **Lower Wick (Lower Shadow):** The thin line extending below the body. Shows the lowest price reached. A long lower wick means sellers pushed price down, but buyers stepped in and pushed it back up.

The **size** of the body relative to the wicks tells you who had conviction. A large body with small wicks = one side dominated completely. A small body with large wicks = neither side could hold control.

## Single-Candle Patterns

### 1. Doji — Indecision

A **Doji** has an extremely small body (Open and Close are nearly equal) with wicks extending both above and below. It looks like a cross or plus sign.

- **What it means:** Neither buyers nor sellers could gain the upper hand. The market is indecisive.
- **Context matters:** A Doji after a strong uptrend suggests the trend may be losing steam. A Doji after a prolonged downtrend can signal a potential reversal.
- **Example:** ETH printing a Doji on the daily chart after 10 consecutive green days is a meaningful warning sign to longs.
- **Subtypes:** A **Gravestone Doji** (long upper wick, almost no lower wick) signals bearish reversal. A **Dragonfly Doji** (long lower wick, almost no upper wick) signals bullish reversal.

### 2. Hammer — Bullish Reversal

A **Hammer** appears at the bottom of a downtrend. It has:
- A small body at the top of the candle
- A long lower wick (at least 2x the length of the body)
- Little to no upper wick

- **What it means:** Sellers pushed price sharply lower during the period, but buyers overwhelmed them and drove price back up to close near the high. Strong buying pressure.
- **Confirmation:** The next candle should be bullish and close above the Hammer's body.
- **Example:** SOL forming a Hammer on the daily chart after dropping to a major support level strongly suggests an incoming bounce.

### 3. Shooting Star — Bearish Reversal

The **Shooting Star** is the opposite of a Hammer. It appears at the top of an uptrend and has:
- A small body at the bottom of the candle
- A long upper wick (at least 2x the length of the body)
- Little to no lower wick

- **What it means:** Buyers pushed price sharply higher, but sellers stepped in aggressively and drove price back down. Buying pressure is exhausted.
- **Confirmation:** The following candle should be bearish, confirming rejection at that level.
- **Example:** BTC printing a Shooting Star at a major resistance zone after a prolonged rally is a classic bearish signal.

### 4. Spinning Top — Indecision with Moderate Range

A **Spinning Top** has a small body positioned in the middle of the candle, with wicks of roughly equal length above and below.

- **What it means:** Both buyers and sellers were active, but neither gained decisive control. Like a Doji, but with a slightly larger body.
- **Context:** At a trend extreme, a Spinning Top suggests the trend could be reversing. In the middle of a trend, it may just be a brief pause.

## Two-Candle Patterns

### 5. Bullish Engulfing — Reversal Signal

A **Bullish Engulfing** pattern consists of:
1. A bearish (red) candle
2. A larger bullish (green) candle that **completely engulfs** the body of the previous red candle — its open is lower than the red candle's close, and its close is higher than the red candle's open.

- **What it means:** Sellers were in control, but buyers came back with overwhelming force and completely reversed the prior session's losses. A powerful reversal signal.
- **Best context:** Appearing after a downtrend, at a support level, with above-average volume.
- **Example:** After ETH drops for five days and hits the $1,500 support zone, a large Bullish Engulfing candle with high volume is a strong long signal.

### 6. Bearish Engulfing — Reversal Signal

The **Bearish Engulfing** is the mirror image:
1. A bullish (green) candle
2. A larger bearish (red) candle that completely engulfs the prior green candle's body.

- **What it means:** Buyers were in control, but sellers came roaring back and wiped out all the prior session's gains.
- **Best context:** Appearing after an uptrend, at a resistance level, ideally with high volume.
- **Example:** BTC hitting $50,000 resistance and forming a large Bearish Engulfing candle is a classic sell signal for short-term traders.

### 7. Tweezer Tops and Bottoms — Equality = Reversal

**Tweezer Tops** occur at the peak of an uptrend: two consecutive candles (one green, one red) with **nearly identical highs**. The market tested that price level twice and rejected it both times.

**Tweezer Bottoms** occur at the base of a downtrend: two candles with **nearly identical lows**. The market tested that support twice and held both times.

- **What it means:** The market is failing to make new highs (or lows), showing the momentum of the current trend is exhausting.
- **Example:** SOL making equal highs at $180 on two consecutive daily candles signals strong resistance at that level.

## Three-Candle Patterns

### 8. Morning Star — Bullish Reversal

The **Morning Star** is a three-candle pattern that signals a potential bottom:
1. **Candle 1:** A large bearish (red) candle — the downtrend is in full force.
2. **Candle 2:** A small-bodied candle (often a Doji or Spinning Top) that gaps below the first candle — sellers are losing momentum.
3. **Candle 3:** A large bullish (green) candle that closes well into the body of Candle 1 — buyers have seized control.

- **What it means:** The downtrend is failing. Sellers exhausted themselves (Candle 1 and 2), and buyers are now dominant (Candle 3).
- **Example:** This pattern appearing on BTC's daily chart after a prolonged bear market has historically preceded significant rallies.

### 9. Evening Star — Bearish Reversal

The **Evening Star** is the bearish equivalent of the Morning Star:
1. **Candle 1:** A large bullish (green) candle — uptrend in force.
2. **Candle 2:** A small-bodied candle that often gaps above the first — buyers are losing energy.
3. **Candle 3:** A large bearish (red) candle that closes well into Candle 1's body — sellers take over.

- **What it means:** The uptrend is stalling. Buyers ran out of steam (Candle 2), and sellers are now pressing hard (Candle 3).
- **Example:** ETH forming an Evening Star on the weekly chart at an all-time high zone is a high-priority warning for holders.

## Using Patterns with Confirmation

Candlestick patterns are **not magic signals** that work every time. They are probability tools. To increase their reliability:

### Volume Confirmation
- The reversal candle (or the third candle in a three-candle pattern) should have **above-average volume**. High volume means many participants are agreeing with the move. A Bullish Engulfing candle on triple the average volume is far more reliable than one on below-average volume.

### Price Context
- **Where** the pattern appears matters enormously. A Hammer at a major support level is far more powerful than a Hammer forming in the middle of nowhere. Always ask: is this pattern appearing at a meaningful price level?

### Higher Timeframe Alignment
- A Bullish Engulfing on the 15-minute chart is much less significant than one on the daily chart. And a bullish pattern on the daily chart is stronger when the weekly chart is also bullish. Trade in the direction of the higher timeframe trend.

### Avoid Trading Every Pattern
- Not every Doji or Hammer is worth acting on. Wait for patterns that appear at key levels, with volume confirmation, in the right market context. Quality over quantity.

## Key Takeaways

> **Lesson 02 — Candlestick Patterns**
>
> - A candle's **body** shows the Open-to-Close range; **wicks** show the High-to-Low range. Long wicks indicate price rejection.
> - **Single-candle patterns:** Doji = indecision; Hammer = bullish reversal; Shooting Star = bearish reversal; Spinning Top = indecision.
> - **Two-candle patterns:** Bullish/Bearish Engulfing signals a strong reversal when one candle completely engulfs the prior candle's body. Tweezer Tops/Bottoms signal trend exhaustion.
> - **Three-candle patterns:** Morning Star (bullish reversal at bottoms) and Evening Star (bearish reversal at tops) are among the most reliable patterns in crypto trading.
> - **Context is everything.** A pattern at a major support/resistance level is far more meaningful than the same pattern appearing in the middle of a range.
> - **Always confirm with volume.** A reversal pattern with high volume has far greater reliability.
> - **Higher timeframe patterns trump lower timeframe patterns.** A daily Bearish Engulfing outweighs a bullish 5-minute signal.
