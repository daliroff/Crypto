# Volume Analysis: The Market's Most Honest Indicator

Price tells you what the market did. Volume tells you how much conviction was behind it. A price move without volume is a whisper; a price move backed by surging volume is a shout. Volume analysis is one of the oldest and most respected disciplines in technical trading, and in crypto markets — where retail sentiment and whale activity can move prices dramatically — understanding volume can give you a crucial edge.

## Why Volume Matters

Every transaction in a market requires both a buyer and a seller. Volume measures the total quantity of an asset that changed hands over a given period. High volume means many participants were actively trading; low volume means the market was quiet and participation was thin.

Volume is important for two fundamental reasons:

**1. It validates price moves.** A breakout above resistance is far more meaningful when accompanied by a large spike in volume. Without volume confirmation, the move is more likely to be a false breakout — a trap that reverses quickly once the small group of participants runs out of buying power.

**2. It reveals supply and demand pressure.** When large buyers accumulate a position, volume rises. When sellers aggressively unload, volume rises. Periods of low volume typically reflect indecision or the absence of conviction on either side.

In crypto, volume data is available for every asset on every exchange and is displayed directly below price charts on TradingView, Binance, and most other platforms as vertical bars — green for periods where price closed up, red for periods where price closed down.

## Volume as Trend Confirmation

The relationship between volume and price direction is the foundation of volume analysis. The core principles:

**Volume should expand in the direction of the trend.** In a healthy uptrend, volume is typically higher on up days than on down days. In a healthy downtrend, volume is higher on down days. When this pattern breaks — when price keeps rising but volume on up days is shrinking — it is an early warning that the trend is losing participation and may be near exhaustion.

**Breakouts need volume.** When price breaks through a significant support or resistance level, experienced traders look immediately at volume. A breakout on twice the average daily volume is a genuine signal. A breakout on below-average volume is suspect and has a much higher probability of failing and reversing back into the range.

**Pullbacks in an uptrend should be low volume.** During a bull market, when price dips back toward support, you want to see low volume — indicating that few people are selling and the pullback is just profit-taking, not distribution. A sharp drop on high volume during an uptrend is a red flag suggesting significant selling pressure has entered the market.

## On-Balance Volume (OBV)

**On-Balance Volume (OBV)** is a cumulative volume indicator created by Joseph Granville in 1963. It adds volume on up-close days and subtracts volume on down-close days, creating a running total that reflects net buying or selling pressure over time.

```
If Close > Previous Close:  OBV = Previous OBV + Volume
If Close < Previous Close:  OBV = Previous OBV - Volume
If Close = Previous Close:  OBV = Previous OBV
```

OBV's power lies in what it reveals about accumulation and distribution:

- **OBV rising while price is flat or rising** = accumulation — smart money is quietly buying.
- **OBV falling while price is flat or rising** = distribution — smart money is quietly selling into strength.
- **OBV diverging from price** is the most actionable signal.

**Bullish OBV divergence:** Price makes a lower low but OBV makes a higher low. This means more volume is coming in on up days than down days despite lower prices — a sign that buyers are absorbing supply and a reversal may be near.

**Bearish OBV divergence:** Price makes a higher high but OBV makes a lower high. Despite the new price highs, net volume pressure is waning — distribution is occurring and the rally may be running out of buyers.

OBV is available on TradingView as a built-in indicator and is one of the most useful free tools for spotting smart money activity before price confirms it.

## Volume Spikes at Breakouts and Reversals

**Volume spikes** — bars that are dramatically larger than surrounding bars — often mark turning points. Two key scenarios:

**Climactic volume at reversals:** When a market has been falling for an extended period and a single candle prints on volume that is 3–5 times the average, it often marks a **selling climax** — the final flush where panicked sellers overwhelm the market and buyers absorb the supply. After the selling climax, price often stabilizes and reverses. Bitcoin's capitulation in June 2022 near $17,500 and again in November 2022 near $15,500 displayed classic climactic volume signatures.

**Volume spike at breakout:** When price breaks above a well-defined resistance level and the breakout candle has volume 2–3 times the 20-day average, the odds of a sustained move are significantly higher than a low-volume breakout. The volume surge indicates that many participants who were waiting for confirmation are now entering simultaneously.

## Low-Volume vs High-Volume Moves

Not all price moves are created equal:

| Scenario | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Price up + High Volume | Strong bullish conviction, likely to continue |
| Price up + Low Volume | Weak rally, possible trap — treat with caution |
| Price down + High Volume | Strong bearish conviction, institutional selling |
| Price down + Low Volume | Routine profit-taking, likely temporary pullback |

Low-volume moves during off-hours (particularly weekend nights in crypto when institutional desks are offline) have a higher tendency to be reversed when high-volume sessions resume. Many experienced traders discount moves that happen in thin, low-liquidity conditions.

## Volume Profile: Where Price Was Accepted

**Volume Profile** is a more advanced concept that plots volume at price levels rather than over time. Instead of vertical bars at the bottom of the chart, volume profile shows horizontal bars that reveal exactly which price levels had the most trading activity.

The key concept within volume profile is the **Point of Control (POC)** — the price level with the highest traded volume over a selected period. The POC acts as a magnet; price tends to revisit these high-activity nodes because they represent levels where buyers and sellers previously reached agreement in large quantities.

**High Volume Nodes (HVN)** are price levels where a lot of volume traded — these tend to act as support or resistance because many participants have positions established there.

**Low Volume Nodes (LVN)** are price gaps where little volume traded — price tends to move through these quickly when revisited, as there are few established positions to act as friction.

Volume Profile is available on TradingView (free and premium versions) and is particularly useful for identifying where price is likely to stall or accelerate.

## Price and Volume Divergence

When price and volume tell different stories, pay attention:

**Rising price + falling volume over multiple weeks** = deteriorating conviction behind the uptrend. This pattern frequently appears in the final stages of bull markets when retail buyers push prices higher but institutional volume is fading.

**Falling price + falling volume over multiple weeks** = the downtrend may be losing momentum. Sellers are exhausted. This often appears before a bottom, as it shows that fewer and fewer participants are willing to sell at lower prices.

Monitoring these longer-term divergences on the weekly chart is one of the most reliable methods for identifying late-stage bull markets and late-stage bear markets.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Volume validates price.** Moves on high volume are more reliable; moves on low volume are more likely to reverse.
- **Breakouts require volume confirmation.** A breakout above resistance on below-average volume has a high probability of being a false breakout.
- In a healthy uptrend, **up days should have higher volume than down days.** When this pattern reverses, the trend is weakening.
- **OBV (On-Balance Volume)** is a cumulative indicator that reveals whether net volume pressure is bullish or bearish — even when price has not yet moved. OBV divergence from price is one of the most powerful early-warning signals in technical analysis.
- **Volume spikes** at price extremes (selling climax, buying frenzy) often mark short-term reversal points.
- **Low-volume moves** — especially during off-hours or thin market conditions — are frequently reversed during high-liquidity sessions.
- **Volume Profile** shows where volume traded at each price level. The Point of Control (POC) acts as a price magnet; high-volume nodes are support/resistance; low-volume nodes are fast-moving zones.
- **Price rising on falling volume over time** is a bearish divergence warning that the rally is losing participation — one of the most reliable signals of a maturing bull move.
