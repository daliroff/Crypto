# Lesson 07.02: Liquidity Pools and Automated Market Makers

## What Is a Liquidity Pool?

A **liquidity pool** is a smart contract that holds two (or more) tokens and enables decentralized trading between them. Instead of matching buyers with sellers via an order book, traders swap directly against the pool's token reserves. The price is determined automatically by a mathematical formula, not by supply and demand from individual market participants.

Anyone can deposit tokens into a liquidity pool to become a **Liquidity Provider (LP)** and earn a share of the trading fees generated by the pool.

This innovation — the **Automated Market Maker (AMM)** — solved one of the hardest problems in decentralized trading: how do you create a functional market without a centralized operator constantly adjusting prices and matching orders?

---

## Traditional Order Book vs. AMM

### Traditional Order Book (CEX Model)

In a centralized exchange like Binance or Coinbase, every trade requires:
- A **buy order** (e.g., "I'll buy 1 ETH at $2,000")
- A matching **sell order** (e.g., "I'll sell 1 ETH at $2,000")
- A central system to match them

This works well when there are many active traders. But on a decentralized blockchain, this model has problems:
- Placing and canceling orders consumes gas (expensive)
- Low-volume pairs have poor liquidity and wide spreads
- Market makers must be online constantly to provide liquidity

### AMM Model

With an AMM:
- A pool smart contract holds, say, 100 ETH and 200,000 USDC
- Any trader can swap against the pool instantly, paying only gas
- The price adjusts automatically based on the ratio of tokens in the pool
- No order book, no matching engine, no counterparty needed

The trade-off is that AMMs can have **price impact** (slippage) on large trades, since trading shifts the token ratio.

---

## The AMM Formula: x * y = k

The foundational formula used by Uniswap (the most influential AMM) is the **constant product formula**:

```
x * y = k
```

Where:
- **x** = quantity of Token A in the pool
- **y** = quantity of Token B in the pool
- **k** = a constant that never changes (the "invariant")

Every trade must keep `x * y` equal to `k`. When you add more of Token A (selling A into the pool), Token B decreases (you receive B from the pool), keeping the product constant.

### Worked Example: Buying ETH from a Uniswap Pool

**Starting state:**
- Pool: 100 ETH + 200,000 USDC
- k = 100 × 200,000 = **20,000,000**
- Implied ETH price = 200,000 / 100 = **$2,000 per ETH**

**A trader buys 10 ETH** (sends USDC to the pool, receives ETH):

After the trade, the pool has 90 ETH. What USDC does the pool need to keep k constant?

```
New USDC = k / New ETH = 20,000,000 / 90 = 222,222 USDC
```

The trader sent **222,222 - 200,000 = 22,222 USDC** to receive **10 ETH**.

**New implied price:** 222,222 / 90 = **$2,469 per ETH**

The price of ETH **increased from $2,000 to $2,469** because the pool now has less ETH. The larger the trade relative to pool size, the more the price moves — this is called **price impact** or **slippage**.

## LP Tokens: Your Share of the Pool

When you deposit assets into a liquidity pool, you receive **LP tokens** (Liquidity Provider tokens) from the smart contract. These tokens represent your proportional share of the pool.

Example:
- Pool total: 100 ETH + 200,000 USDC = $400,000 total
- You deposit: 10 ETH + 20,000 USDC = $40,000 (10% of pool)
- You receive: LP tokens representing **10% of the pool**

When you want to withdraw your liquidity, you return your LP tokens and receive your proportional share of the **current pool reserves** — which may differ from what you deposited due to trades and fee accumulation.

LP tokens are also themselves composable — you can often stake LP tokens in other DeFi protocols to earn additional yield (the basis of yield farming).

---

## How LP Fees Work

Every trade on Uniswap V2 charges a **0.3% fee** on the trade amount. This fee stays in the pool and is distributed proportionally to all LPs.

Example:
- Daily trading volume in ETH/USDC pool: $10,000,000
- Total fee collected: 0.3% × $10,000,000 = **$30,000 per day**
- You own 1% of the pool
- Your daily fee income: **$300 per day**

On an annualized basis, high-volume pools can generate significant APY for LPs. Uniswap V3 introduced **concentrated liquidity**, allowing LPs to provide liquidity only within a specific price range, dramatically increasing fee efficiency for sophisticated LPs.

## Impermanent Loss: The Hidden Cost of Providing Liquidity

**Impermanent loss (IL)** is the most important risk for liquidity providers. It occurs when the relative price of your deposited tokens changes after you deposit them. The pool's rebalancing mechanism means you end up with less of the outperforming asset than if you had simply held.

### Why It Happens

When ETH rises in price, arbitrageurs buy ETH from the pool (where it's cheaper than the market) until the pool price matches the market. This leaves the pool with **less ETH and more USDC** — you've effectively been selling ETH as it rises.

If you had simply held your original 10 ETH + 20,000 USDC, you'd benefit fully from ETH's rise. As an LP, you hold a rebalanced portfolio that underperforms.

### Impermanent Loss Formula

```
IL = 2√(price_ratio) / (1 + price_ratio) - 1
```

Where **price_ratio** = new price / original price

### IL Example: ETH Doubles in Price

- Original ETH price: $2,000
- New ETH price: $4,000
- Price ratio: 4,000 / 2,000 = **2**

```
IL = 2√(2) / (1 + 2) - 1
IL = 2 × 1.4142 / 3 - 1
IL = 2.8284 / 3 - 1
IL = 0.9428 - 1
IL = -0.0572 → approximately -5.7%
```

If ETH doubles, your LP position is worth **5.7% less** than simply holding the original 50/50 ETH and USDC outside the pool. The loss is "impermanent" only if prices return to the original ratio — if you withdraw while the price is up, the loss is permanent.

### IL by Price Change

| Price Change (ETH) | Impermanent Loss |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| 2× (doubles)       | -5.7%           |
| 3× (triples)       | -13.4%          |
| 5×                 | -25.5%          |
| 10×                | -42.5%          |

---

## When LP Fees Outweigh Impermanent Loss

LPs are only profitable when trading fees collected **exceed** the impermanent loss incurred.

**Favorable conditions for LP profitability:**
- **High trading volume** relative to pool size (high fee income)
- **Low price volatility** between the two assets (low IL)
- **Stable pairs**: USDC/USDT, DAI/USDC — the two tokens track the same value, so IL is minimal and fees are near-pure profit
- **Correlated assets**: ETH/stETH (staked ETH) or BTC/WBTC — prices move together

**Unfavorable conditions:**
- One asset moons while the other stays flat (classic volatile pair scenario)
- Low trading volume — fees don't compensate for the rebalancing drag
- Providing liquidity for new, speculative tokens that can crash

**The honest reality:** For most volatile token pairs, impermanent loss erodes returns significantly. Stablecoin pairs (Curve's primary market) and highly correlated asset pairs are generally the most sustainable LP strategies.

> ## Key Takeaways
>
> - A **liquidity pool** is a smart contract holding two tokens that enables instant, permissionless trading via an **Automated Market Maker (AMM)** formula.
> - The **constant product formula x × y = k** governs most AMMs: buying one token reduces its quantity in the pool, raising its price automatically.
> - Example: a pool with 100 ETH + 200,000 USDC (k = 20M). After buying 10 ETH, the pool has 90 ETH + 222,222 USDC — ETH's price rose from $2,000 to $2,469.
> - **LP tokens** represent your proportional share of the pool; return them to withdraw your share of current reserves.
> - Uniswap V2 charges a **0.3% fee per trade**, distributed to LPs proportionally to their pool share.
> - **Impermanent loss (IL)** is the underperformance versus simply holding: if ETH doubles, your LP position is ~5.7% less than if you had held; if ETH 10xs, you lose ~42.5% versus holding.
> - LPs profit when **trading fees exceed impermanent loss** — stablecoin pairs (near-zero IL, pure fee income) are generally the most sustainable LP strategy.
